# The Legend of Alsbans

## Foreword: Once Upon a Time

*A quiet invitation into the old tale of Alsbans.*

Some of you started reading this out of curiosity, some of you do not even know why you are here, and some of you have not read this at all. How do I know? I know from myself. These stories were written by the hands of a young person whose eyes had closed and drifted into dreams. Once upon a time, there was an Alsbans...

## Chapter 1: The Baby Born in Humpellia

*Talis grows up beneath absence, enters Trixalisse, and returns to a village waiting on the edge of war.*

Humpellia, a land of green forests and pink meadows, where people lived on farms and life flowed in quiet happiness. A place where joy felt almost natural, except for one name. Meras. The king known as the punishment of these lands.

In one of Humpellia's most beautiful villages, rain fell heavily as it did every morning. People had opened their windows wide, placed their favorite meals upon their tables, and gathered with those they loved most, laughing and enjoying the day. Rain could not ruin their happiness; in this land, rain meant blessing.

From deep within the forest, the slow sound of hooves echoed along the muddy road. The village governor Uzak was returning from distant lands. Birds sang around him, yet the sorrow in his eyes outweighed every sound.

At the entrance of the village, among the joyful voices of children, he stopped. He took a deep breath and called out into the dim light. "People of my village, gather yourselves. I have an announcement."

At once, everything fell silent. Heads leaned out of windows, some villagers stepped outside with their meals still in hand, while others woke their parents. Though the governor could barely be seen in the darkness, his sadness was felt by all.

He dismounted slowly and walked into the heart of the village. Babies stopped crying. Animals no longer grazed or drank. Sweat formed on his brow as his thoughts tangled. Should he say it now?

Then a voice cut through the silence. "Father." Uzak froze. His son was running toward him. He opened his arms and embraced the boy.

"Where were you? We missed you so much. Mother told me you had gone far away. I was scared."

Looking into his son's eyes, Uzak forced strength into his voice. "Do not worry, my son. Who would dare cause trouble when a governor like me stands here?" His tone hardened. "We will talk later. Go to your mother now."

At last, Uzak stood at the center of the village. Every eye was fixed upon him. "My people, today I bring news from distant lands." Whispers rose as impatience grew. "Say it already," someone murmured.

Uzak drew a deep breath and let the words fall heavily upon the village. "All fathers and all men who participated in the Tree Contest, gather yourselves. We are leaving."

For a moment there was silence. Then voices erupted. "Where? Why? How can this be?" No one understood what was happening, but everyone felt it. Something had changed.

People looked into the eyes of those they loved most. The rain continued to fall, and that morning Humpellia ceased to be the place it had been before.

Everyone mounted their horses. Each went to embrace their loved ones one last time. Uzak returned to his home as well. His son Talis was crying. His wife, trying to remain strong, wrapped her arms around him.

"We will wait for you. Do not forget us." Uzak forced the trembling from his voice. "I promise I will see you again. Not once but ten times if I must. I will always love you."

He kissed his wife one final time and turned to leave, only to find the door locked. When he turned back, he understood. Talis had taken the keys and locked the door from the inside.

"My father is not leaving," the boy cried as he ran through the village streets. "No one will take him from me."

The moment Uzak heard his son's voice, he knew. He broke the door open and rushed outside. Villagers tried to hold Talis back, but the child fought them with all his strength. At last, his father reached him and held his son one final time.

"Don't go," Talis screamed. But he had to go.

"You left me again, Father! Go! You are no longer my father." His mother pulled Talis into her arms. From atop his horse, Uzak looked at his son one last time.

"Whether you hate me or not, the day you become governor, you will understand. We are going to Meras because I love you more than myself." Then he raised his voice. "We are leaving! Everyone mount your horses."

Talis cried in his mother's arms, screaming, "Father, don't go!" But Uzak rode out of the village without looking back. The tears in his eyes sank deep within him, but his heart was burning. His heart and the hearts of all who left. No one looked back. Not even once. Because it was hard. Terribly hard.

![Talis standing before ancient wizards beneath the high trees of Trixalisse.](/images/stories/talis-and-wizards.png)

*In Trixalisse, hope sounded almost like a voice.*

Days turned into weeks and weeks into months. "Mother, it has been a long time since my father left. Why has he still not returned? How many more years must I wait?"

"There is nothing we can do but wait. We must wait, Talis."

Ten years passed. Now Talis was old enough to take part in the Tree Contest. Every boy was sent to distant lands and given only four things: a long rope, a piece of wood, a small paper showing where he would go, and a tiny bottle of poison so that if he were to die, he would die with honor.

All the mothers gathered in the mountains to bid farewell to their sons. "Mother, if I return and my father is here, tell him how long I waited. Tell him to keep the promise he made to me."

"What promise? Your father never promised you anything."

"He did. I know it. I feel it."

Then the announcer spoke loudly. "All mothers, be seated. Know this: the spirits of the fathers are with us. They have always been with us. Today we send our young men away. Today they will learn what it means to be a man. Some of you will return. Some of you will not. Never forget: being a man is not only surviving for years. Being a man is wisdom and strength ruled by wisdom. You may go now."

Each boy embraced his mother and younger siblings one last time. "Do not forget. I am proud of you for who you are." They embraced for the final time and parted with hearts both happy and broken. Each boy walked toward his own land.

When Talis unfolded his paper, he saw the name written upon it: Trixalisse. The road was long. He walked and walked. Each path was harder than the one before. Hunger grew within him. One night, using the rope he had been given, he caught a horned deer in the darkness and killed it. That night he ate well.

At dawn he continued his journey. He crossed mountains and finally saw the towering trees of Trixalisse. They were so tall that their tops could not be seen. After a long journey he arrived in Trixalisse. He was exhausted, but for his father and for those he loved, he had to continue.

"Why is it so dark here? What is that sound?" Voices echoed from everywhere. "Help me, my son! Help me!"

"Father, is that you? Where are you?"

When the flame in his hand went out, he could see nothing. Panic took hold of him. He hid beneath a tree and waited. He did not know whether it was night or morning. But he knew one thing: he could not return without doing something great.

He stood up and shouted. "Leave me alone! I did not come to harm you. I came for my father!"

"Then save me, my son!"

Not knowing where the sound came from, Talis began to run. "Where are you, Father? I am coming!" Suddenly he tripped over a grave and fell. Pain surged through him. He could see nothing, only hear laughter. They were laughing at him.

Talis remained calm and stood up. "Do you think I do not know? Do you think I do not know you are trying to deceive me, that those voices were not my father's? I know. But hope... Hope is like this."

The laughter stopped. "Who are you? Show yourselves."

In an instant the darkness vanished and daylight took its place. Before him stood ugly ancient sorcerers, old and twisted.

"Why have you come here, boy of Humpellia? What business could you possibly have in this place?"

"Hope brought me here."

"You call it hope. I call it poison given for death." And at that moment, the sorcerers liked him.

"Come with us, child."

Talis stared in confusion. "How can you be a wizard without your hands?"

Trakless approached him, bending slightly. "What is your name? Begin with that."

"Leave me alone. I didn't come here to listen to your nonsense."

Another wizard stepped forward. "Did you come to defy us, child? What is your purpose?"

Talis thought carefully. "My aim is to grow, to become strong and brave."

The wizards laughed, all but one. Trakless stared into Talis' eyes, searching for the truth within him.

"Be silent while others laugh," the Grand Wizard said.

Trakless moved closer and asked again. "Why have you come here, Talis?"

"I... I have come for my father. Yes, for my father."

Silence fell. All attention focused on Talis, the Grand Wizard, and Trakless.

"Tell me, what is the name of your greatest power?"

"I don't know," Talis admitted.

Trakless turned to the other wizards. "What is the greatest power?"

"Speed, strength, the mightiest spell," they shouted, but from the back, a quiet voice spoke.

"Wisdom," Talis said.

Trakless smiled and motioned for him to follow. "We will show you around. These tall trees are the finest of Trixalisse."

"Why are they so tall?"

"They are not tall. We are small," Trakless said with a grin.

They reached an ancient hut, eerie and forgotten, home only to the last eight wizards. Deep within Trixalisse, it was a desolate place.

"You will remain here, Talis."

He accepted. Months became years. Talis grew into a strong, experienced man, ready to become a true governor.

He left the wizards behind, never wishing to see them again. The goodness in his heart was not present in them. Returning to his village, he felt excitement and joy. Having conquered the Tree Contest, he was coming back as the future governor, ready to claim his place.

![A cloaked parent lifting the infant Alsbans beneath a field of stars.](/images/stories/alsbans-cover.png)

*When Alsbans was named, a flash of starlight crossed Talis' vision.*

After the bitter cold had passed, Talis finally saw his village in the distance. Because it was night, he chose to rest. He would enter at noon. Lying among the pink meadows, he looked up at the sky.

"What a beautiful star you are. Yes, you, the brightest one. What is your name? I will call you Bans. You are mine now."

Morning came. As he slept upon the pink fields, children from the village noticed him from afar. "Who is that?" they whispered, fear rising in their voices. Slowly, curiosity overcame them and they stepped closer. They thought he was dead. When they approached, they realized he was only sleeping.

As they quietly turned to leave, a pear fell from a tree and struck one of them on the head. A sharp cry broke the silence.

Talis woke abruptly. "What happened? What is going on?" The children ran in fear.

"Do not run. Wait. Ah, never mind."

He stood and continued walking through the beautiful pink meadows. He had not wanted to enter the village at night. Respecting the rules, he stepped into the village at noon.

He walked to the center. "I have returned."

Everyone stared at him. "Who are you?"

"I am Talis. Your governor."

One by one, they gathered. "Welcome, Governor." The crowd grew larger.

"Where is my mother?"

No one answered. Their eyes were filled with sorrow. They led him to a grave.

"When did she die?"

"Five years ago. A sickness."

Talis fell to his knees, tears flowing freely. "Leave me alone. I will stay with my mother."

"Come. Let him be alone."

"Mother, I promise you. I will find my father."

He rose and walked toward his home. When he opened the door, he saw a family inside.

"Who are you?"

"We are the owners of this house, my governor. Your mother gave it to us. We lived together, but we never entered your room. If you wish, we will leave and live elsewhere."

A faint smile appeared on his face. "No. Stay. My room is enough for me."

He entered his old room and lay down. When morning came, no matter how heavy his sorrow remained, he knew he had to be strong. The longing for his mother would never fade, yet he was now the governor of the village. That was his duty.

In time, he married the most beautiful girl in the village. Her name was Melania. She was graceful and brave, the daughter of a noble family. Their wedding was beautiful, a true wedding of love. They moved into a new house and began building their life together.

One day, Melania became pregnant. "I cannot tell anymore, Talis. I think it is a boy."

"No. It is a girl. I can feel it."

"If it is a boy, what will you name him?"

"I do not know. The first name that comes to my heart."

One day, Talis went to the village center to bid farewell to the young men preparing for the new Tree Contest. As he read their names aloud, something felt different. It was as if the pink meadows had grown cold. A quiet sadness settled in every heart.

Then the sound of horses echoed. Forty soldiers of Meras entered the village. Silence swallowed everything.

"Are you Talis?"

"I am Talis."

"We bring a message from Meras."

"I am listening."

"Will the message be spoken before everyone?"

"Yes. I hide nothing from my people."

Anger spread through the crowd. No one loved the men of Meras.

"By order of Meras, in preparation for war against Legacy, all men of Humpellia must-"

Before the sentence was finished, Talis raised his voice. "No one will fight for Meras."

"Yes, no one will go," the people began to shout.

"No one defies Meras. You do not want this, Talis. You know that well."

"No one will go. Tell Meras this. Not a single drop of blood will be spilled."

The village stood united, waiting for a command. The soldiers of Meras reached for their swords. Then a cry rose from afar.

"Talis, you have a son."

Without hesitation, Talis ran toward his home. The soldiers slowly withdrew, retreating into the forest in silence. The entire village gathered, eager to see their future governor.

Talis stepped outside holding his newborn son in his arms. He gently lifted the child before the people. As he looked into his son's eyes, a flash of starlight crossed his vision.

"His name is Alsbans."

Yes. His name is Alsbans.

"Long live Alsbans!" the villagers cried with joy. None of them knew what was coming...

## Chapter 2: The End of Those Who Fear Losing

*Meras returns, Humpellia burns, and the child who survives is carried toward the hidden dark of Trixalisse.*

Morning came, yet no one lived their day in peace. Everyone followed the path where the soldiers of Meras had ridden, searching for them. No one found a trace. What would happen next? No one knew.

"Gather. Everyone, gather. Today is a day to stand strong."

Suddenly, from among the people, several elders stepped forward. "Stop there, Talis."

All eyes turned to the old man who had spoken.

"You know that something great happened yesterday."

"Yes," Talis answered firmly. "My son was born."

"No, Talis," the elder replied. "Yesterday, you betrayed us for your father."

A murmur spread through the crowd.

"I did not act for my father," Talis said, anger rising in his voice. "I acted for my children and for our future."

"No, Talis. I am old. I know what I see. You are young and strong, but Meras... Meras is inevitable. We are only a village. Yes, we have suffered, but there is no escape for us."

Talis clenched his fists. "As long as I stand here, nothing will happen to anyone."

He turned away in anger and returned home.

"What happened, Talis?" Melania asked softly.

"I will never allow anything to happen to you," he said.

"I know," she whispered, embracing him.

They sat together and spoke in low voices. But Talis had already made his plan. What had happened that day was not only the driving away of Meras' soldiers. They had also, in truth, exiled themselves from Humpellia. They would have to go far away.

He knew the soldiers would return. And when they did, they would come in greater numbers. If they stayed, everyone would die. But it had to be done. Otherwise, another generation would grow up without a future, only to be offered to Meras as sacrifice.

Talis gathered the people in the square once more. "My people, you know this does not end here. The soldiers of Meras may have left today, but they will return. And next time, they will come with many more."

"Then what will we do, Talis?" voices shouted.

"They were right yesterday! You sold us for your father!"

"No," Talis roared. "I did this for all of us. Listen to me carefully. If there is wrong, I will carry it. If there is right, I will carry that too. If anyone does not trust me, they are not forced to stand with me. I did not rise for glory. Not for power. Not even for you. I rose for myself, because I refuse to live on my knees. Either fight beside me... or stand against me."

Silence fell. Then something unexpected happened.

"We are with you!" someone shouted. "We have always been with you!"

Talis nodded. "Then take up your swords. At dawn we leave. We will visit our other brothers, speak to the other villages. Every man will prepare for war."

A frightened voice rose from the crowd. "And the women? The children?"

"They will go far away. They will go to Legacy and remain there."

"Legacy?" someone cried. "Are they not our enemies?"

"No," Talis answered calmly. "They are better than we are."

The people returned to their homes to spend what might be their final night beside their loved ones.

"Let me stay with you, Talis," Melania begged. "What am I without you?"

"No," he said gently. "You will raise our son. And you will tell him nothing. I do not want him to grow chasing his father as I did."

She held the baby tightly. "Do you want him to grow without a father? Like you did?" Her tears would not stop.

"Living with hope is always better than living without it," Talis whispered. "I love you, Melania."

They embraced. That night, every house in Humpellia was heavy with sorrow. The people learned that happiness was never permanent. It came and went like the rain. Unknown feelings filled their hearts, feelings they had never known before.

While everyone slept, Talis sat beside his son. He looked into the child's eyes and spoke softly, as if sharing one final father-and-son conversation.

"Take care of your mother, my son. Perhaps one day you will know true happiness."

He kissed his son's forehead. And with one last look, he lay down to sleep. None of them knew that they would awaken to screams that morning...

![Humpellia burning under a sky of smoke while pink meadows darken with firelight.](/images/stories/burning-village.png)

*The pink meadows no longer showed a trace of their former color.*

No one knew where they had come from that night. Meras soldiers laid waste to everything. Houses burning, screams echoing, not a single life spared.

Talis rushed outside, sword in hand, only to see the horror before him: innocent villagers slaughtered mercilessly, chaos reigning.

"Gather, my village!" he shouted, but Melania grabbed his arm, tears streaking her face, pleading him to stay.

"Let me go, Melania. I have to..."

"You will not! Talis, don't you see? It is over. You cannot do anything!"

Meras' soldiers were closing in on Talis' home.

"There they are! Kill!"

"Go, Melania! Take Alsbans and run!"

"But..."

"I said go! Let my heart stay with you!"

"I will not leave you, Talis!"

He held her hands one last time, feeling the weight of their final night together. Melania clung to his feet, sobbing.

"Don't do this, Talis! How can I live without you?"

"For us? No. For Alsbans, my love. For him, I must do this."

Melania rose, kissed him one last time, whispering in his ear, "Revenge will come one day, my love. For you, for all of us, and most of all, for our love, for those who tore us apart."

Cradling Alsbans, she fled into the night, running without a backward glance.

Talis swung his sword with fury, cutting down every Meras soldier who crossed his path, buying precious time for his beloved son. The village fought alongside him, men, women, and elders wielding swords, seeking justice, but Meras' forces were overwhelming.

The blood ran so deep that the pink meadows no longer showed a trace of their former color.

An arrow struck Talis' shoulder, sending him crashing to the ground.

"Father!" he cried, before life left him.

The village burned, a pyre of sorrow. Melania fled with their son, moving silently along empty roads. The soldiers of Meras pursued relentlessly; no one could escape that night.

Exhausted and hungry, they finally reached the mouth of a cavern near Trixalisse. They entered, planning to spend the night hidden, Melania gathering a few bird eggs to feed Alsbans.

But then a voice shattered the darkness.

"Who's there? I'm here, Melania. Do you think you can escape me?"

"Who are you?"

"I am the one who will kill you, Melania. Just as I killed Talis, I will do the same to you..."

"Nooo!" Melania screamed, drawing her sword and striking back.

Wounded and realizing she could not survive, she leaned over the hole where Alsbans was hidden, protecting him with her own lifeless body.

Meras' soldiers had annihilated everyone in the village. Only Alsbans survived. Hidden within the cavern, he cried silently, unheard by anyone.

Except one.

![A single red rose illuminated in a dark cave.](/images/stories/red-rose.jpg)

*A single red rose stood alone among the leaves.*

Alsbans knew nothing. He lay in the dark, crying, until his small body rolled and fell somewhere new. The cave was gone. Around him everything was green, soft and still, like a hidden garden no one had ever found.

He moved his arms and looked around. Then he saw it. A single red rose, standing alone among the leaves. He had never seen anything before, yet he crawled toward it without thinking. It was perfectly smooth, without a single flaw.

When his fingers touched it, the rose went cold for a moment, and the ground swallowed him. He fell again.

It was Jelin who found him. She was walking the path to Trixalisse when she heard the sound beneath the leaves. She pushed them aside and looked down.

"And who is this?" she said softly. She picked him up and turned to the others. "Listen to how frightened he is."

"You are a child yourself, Jelin," one of the wizards said. "How would you care for him?"

"You all care for me. Why not him?"

No one had an answer. Then Trakless appeared. He looked at the child for a long moment and said nothing.

"The child stays," he said finally. "We will look after him."

No one had ever heard such a thing before. They drifted apart in silence. Jelin stepped close to Trakless.

"Why did you agree without asking where he came from?"

"I don't know," he said.

Jelin turned back to Alsbans. She was still a child herself. But she decided, then and there, that she would take care of him.

## Chapter 3: The Long Journey

*Alsbans leaves Trixalisse behind and begins the harder search for a home that will not cast him away.*

Alsbans had learned to walk, and then to talk. From the beginning there was something about him that no one could explain. He understood people. Not their words, but something beneath them, something they had not said and did not plan to. Everyone felt it.

One evening Jelin was crouching beside him, spoon in hand. "Alsbans, you have not eaten. I made this for you."

The door opened. Trakless stepped in and Alsbans moved behind Jelin without a sound.

"He has grown, Jelin. It is time he began working."

"He has only just learned to speak."

"Has he?" Trakless looked at him. "Then let him answer this. Who does he love most?"

Jelin drew a breath. But Alsbans spoke first.

"I love the one who loves me most."

Trakless said nothing. He turned and left.

Jelin looked down at Alsbans for a moment. "Then you love me," she said. "I love you most, Alsbans."

Time passed. And through all of it, there was always something about Alsbans that people felt before they could name it. He was always one step ahead. Not in a way that could be pointed at. Just a feeling that followed him, quiet and constant.

Then the day came. Jelin was running through the trees, laughing.

"You cannot catch me!"

"I am not trying to," Alsbans said.

She stopped. She turned around.

"I want you to run to the edge of the forest. To say goodbye to Trixalisse."

"Not again, Jelin."

"I mean it this time."

He said nothing. She walked to him and put her hand on his shoulder.

"Look at me."

He looked.

"You do not belong here, Alsbans. You are not like us."

He pulled away. "I can be. I will learn, I will do the spells."

"You do not understand," she said. She held his gaze. "You are too good, Alsbans. That is what I mean. You are too good for this place."

That night, while everyone slept, Jelin came to where Alsbans lay and looked into his eyes. She did not know how long she stayed there. But she saw something, the past and what was coming, and when she stood up her mind was made.

A child must leave this place. This child cannot stay.

In the morning she woke him gently and told him where he had been found and how he had come to Trixalisse. They walked together and Alsbans asked the things he had always wanted to ask, about his family, about where they were, about who had taken them from him.

They walked for a long time, past the edges of Trixalisse, farther than they had ever gone together.

"Jelin, we have come very far. We left Trixalisse long ago."

"It is alright, Alsbans. I want to show you something today."

They came to a cave, small and hidden, sealed away from the world. When they stepped inside Alsbans looked around.

"There is nothing here, Jelin."

And then he heard the sound of stone. He turned. Jelin had pushed a great rock across the entrance.

"Jelin. What are you doing? Why did you do this?"

"Listen to me carefully, Alsbans. When you find your way out of here, you will not come back to Trixalisse. Do you understand me?"

"I will come back," Alsbans said, and his voice rose until he was shouting. "I will come back and I will kill you, Jelin. Do not forget that."

Jelin stood on the other side of the rock and said nothing. The disappointment she felt was not in him. It was the opposite. She knew then that her decision had been right.

"Jelin," he called. "Jelin." But there was no answer and no sound.

The cave was dark. A small piece of moonlight came through a crack above him and that was all. On the ground there were a few pieces of food and a little water. Each day Jelin came to the crack and dropped food through and left without a word.

Then one day soldiers of Meras passed nearby and heard him shouting. They came to the rock and called out.

"Who is inside? What is your name?"

Alsbans said, "My name is Kales."

They opened the rock to take him as a slave, but Alsbans came at them and the soldiers of Meras could not bring down a child who fought the way he fought.

But they had another idea. When Alsbans retreated into the cave they gathered pieces of wood and fed them through the crack and set them alight.

Alsbans found a place near the back where the stone was not solid and dug with his hands until the wall gave way and he pulled himself out into the open air.

The soldiers of Meras walked away believing he was dead. Alsbans came out torn and burnt and exhausted and began walking slowly in the direction of Trixalisse. Every step hurt.

That night Jelin came. She moved the rock and called his name and looked inside and saw the burnt ground and the blood and the body of a dead Meras soldier whose burns she took for Alsbans. She began to cry before she had finished looking.

She ran. When she reached Trixalisse she was still crying. Alsbans had just arrived at the edge of the trees. He heard her voice and stopped and stepped behind them and listened.

"Alsbans is dead," Jelin said. "I saw him burning."

Trakless looked at her. The other wizards looked at each other.

"He was already finished for us," someone said.

They drifted apart. Some said nothing. Some smiled. Jelin stood among them crying and no one stayed.

Alsbans watched all of it from the trees. He stood there for a long time. Then he turned and walked away from Trixalisse. He needed to find a new home.

![A dark coastline beneath a vast fading celestial light.](/images/stories/nasa.jpg)

*He walked like a person with somewhere to be.*

He ran. Into the trees, into the dark, screaming.

"Traitors," he screamed. "You are all traitors."

He ran until his lungs burned and his legs had nothing left and still he did not stop. He was hungry and thirsty and he knew it and he did not care. The pain was something to hold onto. It was the only thing that felt honest.

Then he caught his foot on a root and went down hard. He lay there with his face against the ground.

"I don't want anyone anymore," he said to no one. "I don't want to be unhappy anymore."

He pulled the leaves over himself and curled beneath a tree and closed his eyes. In the morning the hunger woke him. He could see smoke somewhere far off through the trees. He stood and walked toward it.

He was angry still but he wanted to live. He walked like a person with somewhere to be, through land that belonged to no one, farther than he had intended to go.

Then he found a fountain and ran to it and drank with both hands. A man appeared beside him. He smelled of the road and his clothes were torn and his eyes were sharp.

"Who are you?" the man said. "Get away from here. This water is mine."

"Why is it yours?" Alsbans said without thinking.

Then he stopped. The man had used his name. He grabbed him by the throat.

"How do you know my name? How do you know it?"

"I heard it," the man said. "In the forest. You were shouting. How could I not know it? Why are you so angry?"

Alsbans let go. He stepped back and looked at the fountain.

"Take it. It is yours."

He turned to leave. The man called after him.

"Alsbans."

"What?"

"Will you tell her that I love her very much?"

Alsbans turned around. "Who?"

But the man was already running and then he was gone. Alsbans watched the empty road for a moment.

"Madman," he said, and kept walking.

The smoke led him to a small settlement, ten people at most, quiet and tucked between the trees. He crouched behind a bush and watched. What he did not know was that they had already seen him.
